---
title: Domains
---

Domains function as a namespace designed to segment and constrain data and operations into designated areas.

They are used to manage complexity and lower the risk associated with granting permissions in large systems by enforcing boundaries.

<Callout type="note">
    If domains are not used, all operations and data are considered global and flat. 
</Callout>

## Key Benefits

Domains provide structural benefits across data management and access control:

- Data Separation and Constraint: Domains segment different parts of a complex system, ensuring that participants (users or organizations) cannot access data or operate outside their specific domain.

- Organizational Management: You can use Domains to manage different organizations within a system, limiting each organization to its own set of data.

- Policy and Rule Enforcement: Domains allow you to apply different rules, policies, and routes to different subsets of data.

- Risk Mitigation: If you grant a user unlimited permissions within a domain, the risk is contained because the system guarantees the user cannot perform actions outside that specific domain.
