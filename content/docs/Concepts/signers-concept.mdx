A Signer represents a participant (you, your system, or your client) that is authorized to submit operations to the Ledger. The process of signing requests ensures that the system can verify the source and integrity of every operation.

In practice, a Signer is defined by a cryptographic key pair. 

## Why Signing Matters

Every operation must be cryptographically signed to maintain the Ledger's secure, auditable environment for financial data. This signature provides:

- Proof of Identity and Data Integrity: The cryptographic signature verifies the sender's identity and confirms that the request payload (e.g., payment amount, destination) remains unchanged from submission.

- Immutable Audit Trail: Tying every successful operation to a verified Signer ensures a complete, immutable audit trail, establishing non-repudiation (the Signer cannot deny the action).


<Callout type="warn" title="WARNING">
  The Ledger will reject any request that is not signed by an authorized Signer.
</Callout>


## Signing Workflow

A Signer is the identity you use to authenticate and cryptographically sign requests when interacting with the Ledger APIs.  

This role ensures every interaction is both secure and verifiable:

<Steps>
<Step>
### Authentication
You first authenticate to prove your identity to the system.
</Step>

<Step>
### Request Signing
You then sign the request payload, guaranteeing the request originates from you and has not been altered in transit.
</Step>

<Step>
### Verification
The Ledger validates the signature against your authenticated identity; if successful, the operation proceeds, otherwise the request is rejected.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Authentication vs. Authorization

The Ledger uses a two-phase security check when it receives a signed request to ensure both identity and permission are valid.

### Phase 1: Authentication

1. You use your private key to generate a unique digital signature for your request.

2. When the Ledger receives the request, it uses your public key to perform authentication. It validates the signature to prove two things:

            - The request was signed by the holder of the matching private key or;
            - The request data has not been altered.

### Phase 2: Authorization

3. Once you are authenticated, the Ledger performs authorization to check if your Signer has the necessary permissions to perform the requested operation. 

    These permissions are defined by two other Minka concepts:

            * Policies: These are the rules that define what actions are allowed (e.g., "can create a wallet," "can transfer funds").
            * Circles: These are groups that link Signers to specific Policies.

4. Your request is only accepted if your signature is valid (authentication) and your Signer is part of a Circle that has a Policy granting permission for the action (authorization).
