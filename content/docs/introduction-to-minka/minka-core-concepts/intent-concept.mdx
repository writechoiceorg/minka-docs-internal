title: Intents
---

An Intent represents a formal request to perform a financial action. It is the necessary step a client takes, often via a single API call, to initiate an operation.

When you create an Intent, you send the operation to the system for processing, meaning the system resolves the final outcome (success or failure) asynchronously later. The ability to create an Intent is central to the End-to-End [Transaction Flow](../transaction-flow.mdx).

## Intents & Claims

The concept of Intent is closely linked to Claims, which provide the specific details of the actions being requested.

- **The Intent (The Container):** Represents the user's purpose or desire for the action (e.g., "I want to pay $100").

- **The Claims (The Actions):** Individual, verifiable statements within the Intent that describe the steps the system must execute (e.g., "Debit account A by $100" and "Credit account B by $100").

The relationship between Intents and Claims is critical for ensuring data integrity:

- **Atomicity:** An Intent guarantees atomicity, meaning that all associated Claims must either all succeed or all fail together. 

<Callout>
    This prevents partial, incomplete, or inconsistent operations in the system.
</Callout>

## Application of Claims

Claims are essential in scenarios where multiple actions must be finalized together to maintain financial accuracy:

- **Taxes & Fees:** If a payment requires collecting a tax or fee, multiple Claims are used. The system ensures that if the primary payment is successfully recorded, the tax debit must also be recorded. This prevents the primary payment from occurring without the fee being collected.

- **Currency Exchanges:** For currency exchange (e.g., giving $100 and receiving €80), placing both parts into two claims ensures that the exchange is atomic, meaning the user is guaranteed to receive the euros if they provide the dollars.
